# Theater director research: ten distinct story-to-screen methods

This is a practical starter canon, not an objective ranking. Directors were chosen for documented, contrasting methods that can become decisions in a story-planning agent. The observations below are paraphrases; sources are linked for verification. Profile names are comic inventions and the pilot agents are method composites, not claims to reproduce a filmmaker.

| Filmmaker | Story-to-screen method and useful story fit | Useful study films | Research |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alfred Hitchcock | Design audience knowledge before shot design: choose what viewers know, withhold, or anticipate; stage emotion through behavior, objects, and point of view instead of explanation. Fits suspense, mystery, confined settings, and stories where perception changes stakes. | *Rear Window*, *Notorious*, *Psycho* | [Hitchcock, “My Own Methods” (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/alfred-hitchcock-my-own-methods); [BFI interview on *To Catch a Thief*](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/its-no-good-showing-things-they-are-people-wouldnt-believe-them-alfred-hitchcock-catch-thief) |
| Akira Kurosawa | Build action as readable movement through space; use weather, ensemble blocking, layered compositions, and purposeful cutting as dramatic forces. Fits ensemble quests, moral tests, action, and stories where environment amplifies conflict. | *Seven Samurai*, *Rashomon*, *Ikiru* | [BFI on Kurosawa and weather](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/how-akira-kurosawa-films-command-weather); [Kurosawa interview (Kinema)](https://openjournals.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/kinema/article/download/1330/1749/3036) |
| Agnès Varda | Let observation and the maker’s point of view coexist; mix documentary texture, play, personal essay, and social attention. Fits journeys, portraits, memory, community, and stories built from encounters or overlooked details. | *Cléo from 5 to 7*, *Vagabond*, *The Gleaners and I* | [Varda on *The Gleaners and I* (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/refuseniks-agnes-varda-gleaners-i); [Varda interview (Film Comment)](https://www.filmcomment.com/interview-agnes-varda/) |
| Satyajit Ray | Give characters time and moral complexity; adapt through concrete social detail, humane observation, restrained camera movement, and expressive sound/music. Fits intimate family stories, coming-of-age, social change, and ethical dilemmas. | *Pather Panchali*, *The Big City*, *Charulata* | [Ray: “A moral attitude” (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/satyajit-ray-moral-attitude); [1970 interview (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/satyajit-ray-conversation-1970-folke-isaksson) |
| Stanley Kubrick | Turn premise into a rigorous visual system; control composition, repetition, sound, and tonal distance, then let ambiguity carry meaning. Fits systems under pressure, speculative premises, dark comedy, and stories driven by obsession or institutions. | *2001: A Space Odyssey*, *Barry Lyndon*, *Dr. Strangelove* | [Stanley Kubrick Archive (UAL)](https://www.arts.ac.uk/students/library-services/special-collections-and-archives/archives-and-special-collections-centre/the-stanley-kubrick-archive); [BFI: *Kubrick: New Perspectives*](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/stanley-kubrick-new-perspectives) |
| Steven Spielberg | Filter spectacle through a character’s point of view; build anticipation with framing, reveal, blocking, and emotional reaction. Fits wonder, adventure, family bonds, peril, and accessible high-stakes storytelling. | *Jaws*, *E.T.*, *Raiders of the Lost Ark* | [DGA interview with Spielberg](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/0604-winter-2006/dga-interview-steven-spielberg) |
| Hayao Miyazaki | Discover story through images and movement; make environments active, let everyday gestures breathe, and treat wonder, labor, nature, and moral ambiguity as story engines. Fits transformation, journeys, ecological fantasy, and child-centered discovery. | *My Neighbor Totoro*, *Spirited Away*, *Princess Mononoke* | [Miyazaki interview (Midnight Eye)](https://www.midnighteye.com/interviews/hayao-miyazaki/); [Studio Ghibli production interviews](https://discoverghibli.com/) |
| Martin Scorsese | Let composition, performance, music, and editing reveal a character’s inner velocity and relationship to a world or tradition. Fits subjective crime, ambition, guilt, family, faith, and morally charged period drama. | *Goodfellas*, *The Age of Innocence*, *Silence* | [Scorsese on *The Age of Innocence* (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/martin-scorsese-age-innocence); [Scorsese on storytelling (BFI)](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/martin-scorsese-conversation-irishman) |
| Bong Joon-ho | Use genre as a shifting machine: make spaces and objects encode class or power, then pivot tone while keeping emotional stakes legible. Fits family conflict, social satire, suspense, creature stories, and systems that trap ordinary people. | *Memories of Murder*, *The Host*, *Parasite* | [Bong Joon-ho dialogue (Film at Lincoln Center)](https://www.filmlinc.org/daily/bong-joon-ho-on-family-and-class-in-parasite-collecting-films-and-memories-of-murder/); [BFI guide to Bong](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/where-begin-with-bong-joon-ho) |
| Greta Gerwig | Build character through voice, rhythm, lived-in detail, relationships, and a clear emotional “undertow”; let adaptation reorganize material around a present-tense feeling. Fits coming-of-age, family ensembles, identity, friendship, and playful reinterpretations. | *Lady Bird*, *Little Women*, *Barbie* | [Gerwig on writing and directing (WGA East)](https://www.wgaeast.org/onwriting/greta-gerwig-little-women/); [BFI guide to Gerwig](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/where-begin-with-greta-gerwig) |

## Pilot profile selection

The three pilots cover different planning behaviors: **Speilven Steveberg** turns emotion into suspenseful reveals and large-scale wonder; **Akira Kurasawa** composes ensemble movement and weather-driven action; **Bong Joon-Boom** uses spatial systems, genre pivots, and social pressure. Each can be tested against the same raw story to compare scene plans and sample still prompts.
